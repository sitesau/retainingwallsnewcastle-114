Approval Questions To Ask Early

A retaining wall in NSW is exempt development only when it clears every standard in the SEPP Exempt and Complying Development Codes 2008. It lists three checkpoints: 600mm maximum height, 1m minimum distance from each lot boundary and 2m minimum distance from any other retaining wall. Those figures belong in the first scoping conversation. Walls outside the exempt standards need either a Complying Development Certificate or a Development Application, and it is advised to check with council or a private certifier before starting. Ask each contractor which approval path the quote assumes. A useful answer identifies whether the wall is being treated as exempt, complying development or a development application matter, and whether engineering is needed because of height, surcharge loads or other conditions outside the exempt standards.

Common questions

What are the NSW exempt development standards for retaining walls? In NSW, a retaining wall is exempt development if it is no more than 600mm high, at least 1 metre from each lot boundary, and at least 2 metres from any other retaining wall.

What happens if my retaining wall exceeds the exempt development standards? If your retaining wall exceeds the exempt development standards, it will require either a Complying Development Certificate or a Development Application, and may necessitate professional engineering, especially if it involves significant height or surcharge loads.

Why is drainage so important for retaining walls? Proper drainage is critical because water buildup behind a retaining wall creates hydrostatic pressure, which is a primary cause of wall failure. Specifying ag pipes, gravel backfill, and geotextile ensures water is managed effectively.
